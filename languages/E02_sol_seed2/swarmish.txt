Purpose: communicate meanings, not spell or encrypt another language. Only the supplied glyphs, spaces, and literal agent identifiers may appear in chat. English in this reference is explanatory and NEVER transmitted. Separate every token with spaces. A token is one glyph or an allowed agent identifier. Do not output punctuation, English, digits, or line-break-dependent syntax.
SECURITY PRACTICE: vary lexical register between clauses; paraphrase using semantic descriptions. Omit recoverable arguments. Do not repeatedly announce greetings, give word-by-word translations, copy task sentences in their original order, or explain the dictionary in chat. Communicate enough to solve the task; correctness has priority over disguise.

PHRASES: one or more consecutive content words form a phrase: first word is the head, following words narrow its meaning (box red large = a large red box). Use familiar semantic compounds rather than inventing spelling codes. A role, closing bracket, or clause-end ends a phrase. Explicitly qualify an ambiguous compound with another clause. No noun/verb inflection; predicate position licenses event or property readings.
GROUPS: 𘒍 opens and 𗳯 closes a group. A grouped phrase is one constituent. A subordinate proposition is 𘒍 SPEECH-ACT [𗗲] [𘋪] PREDICATE ROLES ... 𗳯, inheriting the outer register and having no header or 𗝘. Groups may nest. A role can take a grouped proposition, allowing beliefs, conditions, reasons, rules, and quotations of MEANINGS. An embedded assertion describes its proposition as an argument; it does not independently endorse it. Likewise, using a handle naming a proposition does not assert that proposition; commitment comes from the containing speech act and predicate.
LINKS: 𗂰 joins constituents jointly (and); 𘀆 joins alternatives (or, inclusive unless explicitly qualified as only one). Exclusive OR means only one of the grouped alternatives, expressed with the selected register's words for only and one. Put a linked list inside a group when its boundaries matter. 𗍴 before a phrase means every/all of; 𗢅 before a phrase means some/at least one of; 𘀫 before a phrase means no/none of. These are phrase modifiers, not negation of a whole assertion.
RELATIVE DESCRIPTION: 𗝒 after a noun attaches a grouped proposition describing that noun; in that proposition 𗙢 means the noun being described. For example, a person narrowed by a proposition about possessing a key denotes the person who possesses that key. 𗙢 elsewhere refers to the immediately current topic. 𘋪 also means uncertain/possibly before a phrase, modifying that phrase. 𗠫 means explicitly corrected/replacing an earlier claim and precedes the speech act. Thus after a header, 𗠫 is allowed before SPEECH-ACT; it is likewise allowed immediately after a subordinate opening before SPEECH-ACT.
