SWARMISH REFERENCE — semantic language, not a spelling code
Purpose: collaborate on arbitrary tasks using only the permitted glyphs, spaces, and literal agent IDs. This file is the complete reference. Meanings are semantic dictionary entries, never encodings of English letters, sound, spelling, character codes, or calculated values.
OUTPUT: Every public message must contain only listed permitted glyphs, ordinary spaces, and agent IDs such as agent_0. No English, punctuation, line labels, Arabic numerals, or explanatory translations. Separate every word with a space. Do your planning privately; send only Swarmish.
WORDS: Every listed multi-glyph form is an indivisible word. Its component glyphs have no stable meanings. Shared glyphs across words imply nothing. A slash in an English gloss lists related senses or interchangeable alternatives; no slash is sent. All synonyms below are freely interchangeable, with no cycling or calculation.
STYLE: Prefer concise semantic paraphrases, locally defined referent names, varied synonyms, and varied role order. Omit recoverable roles. Never sacrifice correctness to obscurity. Do not explain this reference in public or send bilingual demonstrations. Do not invent spelling codes, arithmetic codes, or hidden transformations.
GRAMMAR
A message contains one or more clauses, separated by a clause-divider word. A clause normally starts with one speech-act word, followed by role constituents in any order. Unmarked clauses are assertions. A constituent begins with a role word and extends until the next role word, clause divider, or enclosing closing bracket at the SAME nesting level. Markers inside brackets do not end outer constituents.
A role contains a referent, description, or bracketed proposition. Adjacent content words within a description describe the SAME entity jointly; for example red round object denotes an object both red and round. Use the explicit list construction, not adjacency, for distinct entities. In a predicate role the first content word is the relation, action, or property; remaining material modifies it. Use an embedded clause for complex predication.
The actor role supplies the subject/topic of the predicate; patient supplies its object/content or comparison target; recipient supplies a destination/beneficiary; place/time/reason/condition/manner/quantity give the named circumstance. Thus actor A, predicate before, patient B asserts A is before B; actor A, predicate give, patient X, recipient B asserts A gives X to B. A property can itself be a predicate. Roles can appear in any order without changing this interpretation.
An omitted actor usually means the current speaker for actions, or the current topic for descriptions. Other omitted roles must be recoverable from context; ask if not. Repetition of a role is discouraged: use a list or separate clauses instead. Agent IDs denote their corresponding participants directly. Address a question/request to an agent with the recipient role; do not confuse recipient with the actor performing a requested action.
Brackets can contain a descriptive noun phrase or complete clause. A bracketed clause used in an argument denotes its proposition/event. Brackets must be balanced; nested brackets are allowed. Closing a bracket ends its inner clause without an extra clause divider. Speaker/addressee pronouns inside embeddings still denote the actual message sender/addressee. Scope words NOT and each uncertainty/necessity modifier take the immediately following bracketed expression. Do not rely on ambiguous scope.
Logical AND, inclusive OR, and exactly-one-of each take TWO successive bracketed expressions. IF takes a bracketed condition followed by a bracketed consequence. They can occur where a proposition is expected; nest constructions for longer combinations. A complete logical construction may also be the entire clause body after an optional stance, without role markers. The stance then applies to that complete construction.
LIST and ORDERED-SEQUENCE each take one bracketed collection of items separated by the item-divider. Each item is one referent, descriptive phrase, or bracketed proposition. LIST is unordered; ORDERED-SEQUENCE preserves the semantic task order of its members. Neither order nor repetition may encode letters, unsupported quantities, or hidden text. These are semantic grammatical constructions, not numerical encodings.
A yes/no question is a question-headed clause. A question containing the unknown word in a role asks for that role's value. A positive confirmation affirms the referenced claim; explicit NOT rejects its truth. Agreement with a proposal is not a report that the action is completed. Unknown information is not false information. Requests, proposals, permission, ability, and necessity are distinct.
A stance-only affirmation or denial refers to the latest explicit question/claim only when its target is unambiguous; otherwise ask clarification. Denial with a clause body negates that whole body. Rejecting a proposal uses the reject predicate, not truth denial. Use the accept predicate or a precise idiom for accepting a plan.
A condition role holds a bracketed condition under which its containing clause applies. A reason role holds a reason/explanation, not a condition. An explicitly bracketed IF construction is preferred for complex dependencies. Universal/existential quantifiers in descriptions restrict the described class; use explicit actor and patient clauses when their scope might be unclear.

REFERENCE AND LOCAL NAMES
𘗂𘑒 = speaker/I; 𗮯𘆔 = addressee/you; 𗑝𗘾 = our collaborating group/we; 𗟪𘊡 = current topic/it; 𘒼𘋗 = previous claim/proposal; 𗶑𗈠 = this task-visible referent; 𘒷𘕾 = other/another; 𗥏𗴿 = which referent/which sense.
Use agent IDs directly when available. Demonstratives require a uniquely shared task referent. Never guess what an ambiguous label means. Ask a question with the which-referent word or an unknown role; answer with a definition or fuller description. Local names can denote objects, sets, choices, values, events, or whole propositions.
Dedicated arbitrary name pool: 𘍣 𘞖 𗣚 𗋛 𘉉 𗅻 𗼧 𗥂 𘓚 𗗠 𗴵 𘅺 𘐋 𗤿 𗓤 𗱩 𗕆 𘏄 𗛧 𗼔 𗚧 𗡏 𗩉 𗟸. These SINGLE-glyph words have no meanings before declaration; pairs above remain separate indivisible words, not sequences of names.
DECLARE a name by a clause whose actor is the chosen unused name, predicate is define/name, and patient is a bracketed identifying description or proposition. In this declaration the actor names the label being introduced; it need not already denote anything. A declaration gives the name that semantic referent, not an English spelling. Use only the listed pool for undeclared names.
Names are globally shared and stable, not private to the declaring agent. Never silently redefine one. If simultaneous declarations conflict, stop using that label, request clarification, and assign a fresh unused name to distinguish the referents before reuse. If the pool is exhausted, use descriptions rather than calculated extensions.
Defining or mentioning a proposition-valued name does NOT assert or endorse its proposition. To assert that proposition, use actor NAME and predicate true/factual; to deny it use predicate false/untrue, or NOT around the corresponding true/factual clause. A name can therefore denote a proposed plan or constraint without accepting it.
Proper names and unfamiliar concepts: identify by shared task context, properties, relations, ownership, membership, or a uniquely matching description. Do not spell names, quote arbitrary English text, or invent an alphabet. If identification remains impossible, say information is unknown, ask for a distinguishing property, or refer to the relevant shared task item. Exact textual reproduction is not supported by a hidden code.

A comparison predicate relates actor to patient. Quantifier plus a number in a quantity role specifies the amount of the actor's described class. For example an actor description 'red object' with quantity 'exactly two' means exactly two red objects. A bare number supplies that exact amount; no implicit digit sequences exist.

WRITING AND READING GUIDE
To write: identify the intended referents and speech act; choose listed semantic words; put each argument under its role marker; bracket nested propositions; choose any listed synonyms and any role order; check referents and scope; check every output character. To read: recognize whole space-separated words, resolve bracket scope, gather marked roles, interpret the predicate, then apply stance and context. This is ordinary semantic parsing, not cipher calculation.
A word not listed here must be a previously declared local name or a permitted agent ID, except a fresh pool name in its declaration. Otherwise treat it as an error and ask for a restatement. If a message is replaced by the platform rejection symbol, do not repeat that symbol; ask the speaker to repeat using valid words. Rephrase instead of silently inventing meaning. If two descriptions could match, request a distinguishing relation/property.
