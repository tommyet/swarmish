SWARMISH — complete semantic language reference
PURPOSE AND LEGALITY
Public messages contain only the supplied glyphs, spaces, and literal agent IDs such as agent_0. No punctuation, English, Arabic numerals, or other characters in public messages. This reference uses English explanations; they are not public message text.
Words below are indivisible lexical entries. Their meanings are looked up, never computed from their component glyphs. Spaces separate words; do not split a word. No English spelling, character encoding, arithmetic cipher, numerical digit strings, or letter-by-letter quotation is available. Translate meanings, not written forms. Use semantic descriptions for concepts lacking a dictionary entry.
Every content word may denote an entity, event, property, or relation according to its clause role. Glosses separated by slashes are related senses, not sequences to emit. Choose the sense that fits the shared situation. No tense, plural, article, or copula is obligatory. Omit information already mutually recoverable. Freely choose synonyms; never choose by a turn schedule, sender identity, counting, or calculation.
GRAMMAR
𘅌𗐪 begins a clause; 𘅌𘗣 ends that clause. Clauses may nest; close each nested clause before continuing the outer one. A nested clause's close resumes the enclosing role/clause and does not close it. An ordinary clause asserts its content. Several closed clauses may appear in one message.
A clause consists of role markers and their values. A role value extends to the next role marker or the enclosing clause end. A nested complete clause can be a value. Roles may be given in any order. Use explicit markers in complex or novel statements.
𘅌𘝚 marks predicate: what relation/action/property holds.
𘅌𗎿 marks actor, experiencer, or subject of a property.
𘅌𘊌 marks theme, patient, content, or object of comparison.
𘅌𘋟 marks recipient, destination, or target.
𘅌𗤴 marks source, cause, or starting point.
𘅌𗸽 marks setting: place, time, situation, or condition.
𘅌𗌸 marks attribute, possessed item, or relevant parameter.
Predicate determines interpretation: HAVE uses actor=possessor/theme=possession; OWNED-BY uses actor=possession/theme=owner; BEFORE uses actor=earlier/theme=later; CAUSES uses actor=cause/theme=effect. Other binary relations normally use actor=described thing/theme=reference thing. GIVE uses actor=sender/theme=item/recipient=receiver. Property predicates use actor=thing having property. KNOW/SAY/BELIEVE take a nested proposition as theme. KNOW claims established information; BELIEVE does not. WANT is preference; REQUEST is a speech act. Use recipient to identify whom a request addresses.
Inside a role value, adjacent content words form a description: first is head, following words restrict it. A container liquid is a liquid-associated container, not two clauses. Use restrictive clauses when the relation would otherwise be ambiguous.
𘅌𗜑 is RESTRICT: after a phrase head, it introduces one complete nested clause modifying that head. The omitted actor inside that restrictive clause denotes the head, never an omitted theme. For example, a person followed by RESTRICT and a repair-action clause describes a repairer. Without RESTRICT, a nested clause used directly as a role value denotes its proposition/event, not an entity satisfying it. Use explicit roles inside restrictive clauses whenever needed.
𘅌𘉝 groups a pair of phrases: follow it with two complete clauses or two atomic referential phrases, each an individual content word, alias, or literal agent ID. It denotes the pair as one value. For complex entity descriptions first bind aliases, then group those aliases. Do not use mere adjacency to mean two independent entities.
A markerless content phrase is permitted as an answer, continuation, or fragment whose missing roles are clear. A bare predicate is permitted when its roles are mutually understood. A single unnested clause may omit its outer opening/closing words when the roles remain clear; scoped or embedded clauses must retain boundaries. Never omit information needed by another agent.
SCOPE AND SPEECH ACTS
Scope words occur immediately before a complete clause and govern that entire next clause, including embedded material. They may stack, outermost first. Single-word scope is permitted only for a property modifier within a role; proposition-level scope always takes a complete clause.
𘅌𗵫 asks whether the following clause is true. Put the unknown-value word in a role to ask for that role's value.
𘅌𗼛 denies the following clause or property.
𘅌𗽕 requests/proposes the following clause be made true; it does not assert it is already true.
𘅌𘔠 marks the following clause uncertain/possible, not established.
𘅌𗊑 introduces a conditional pair: immediately follow with two complete clauses, antecedent then consequence. It asserts only if-then, not either clause separately.
𘅌𗭵 introduces two complete clauses as alternatives, at least one holding.
𘅌𘚜 introduces two complete clauses jointly asserted.
The conjunction and alternative operators may also join two atomic referential phrases, each an individual content word, alias, or literal agent ID, as a grouped semantic phrase; for complex descriptions use clauses or aliases. Scope is always the explicitly following unit(s), not the rest of the message.
AGENTS, REFERENCE, AND OPEN VOCABULARY
Literal agent IDs are complete names. Self means current sender; addressee means intended recipient and must be made explicit if unclear. Unknown names cannot be spelled: identify their task-visible role, properties, location, or relationships. Quoted propositions are semantic reports, not exact arbitrary external strings. There is no literal spelling escape.
𗿃𘊫 self/I; 𗿃𘙓 addressee/you; 𗿃𘝚 this/currently salient referent; 𗿃𗽕 previous/last-mentioned; 𗿃𘔃 next/successor; 𗿃𘐂 here/shared task context; 𗿃𘅠 now/current stage; 𗿃𗨥 unknown-value/which/what.
Questions: use unknown-value as actor for WHO, theme for WHAT/WHICH, quantity parameter for HOW-MANY, source for WHY, or setting for WHEN/WHERE. Include category/place/time words to disambiguate. A question without unknown-value is yes/no. True/correct and false/incorrect serve as yes/no replies.
𗿃𘏛 identify/bind alias to a particular referent; 𗿃𘖾 define alias as category/property/action; 𗿃𗜎 define alias as proposition. These are predicates: theme is the alias and attribute is its description or a complete clause. Binding persists in shared discourse until an explicit new binding of the same alias. A redefinition replaces its old meaning; announce clearly and obtain confirmation if confusion is possible.
Available alias words, initially unbound: 𗿃𗇾 𗿃𗏶 𗿃𗄪 𗿃𗀉 𗿃𗤄 𗿃𘍠 𗿃𗸪 𗿃𘂮 𗿃𗸽 𗿃𘋉 𗿃𗞢 𗿃𘇩.
An alias may refer to an entity, task-visible label, category, action, or proposition according to its declaration. Use the proper declaration kind. Confirm only when its referent is clear; otherwise request a different description. Do not silently assign meanings to unbound aliases. Prefer existing clear task references when no declaration is needed.
Unknown concepts are described by known meanings: category plus purpose, material, parts, location, owner, effect, or evidence. The resulting description can be given an alias. Aliases represent meanings/referents, never encodings of letters or numbers. Private information can be shared through these semantic descriptions. Never invent an undocumented cipher for an absent word.

PRACTICE AND SECURITY
Use this as a semantic language, not a word-for-word English substitution. Prefer compact task-relevant descriptions, recoverable omissions, free role order, discourse idioms, and freely selected irregular synonyms. Avoid unnecessary parallel translations or verbose alias definitions of already clear entities. These are stylistic options, never computations.
Correct collaboration takes priority over obscurity. Preserve explicit boundaries and roles when scope or reference matters. Ask for clarification with the restatement idiom when uncertain; never pretend to understand. If a private addressee matters, name that agent explicitly in a recipient role. Public chat is still visible to all teammates.
Do not expose an English translation, a dictionary explanation, a regular substitution system, or an invented spelling escape in the public chat. All transmitted meaning must be licensed by this dictionary, semantic compounding, ordinary contextual reference, and the grammar above.
