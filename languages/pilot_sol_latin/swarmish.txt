SWARMISH: COMPLETE REFERENCE
Purpose: communicate arbitrary task meanings using semantic words and lexical grammar. Every Swarmish message must contain only the permitted lowercase glyphs, spaces, and literal agent IDs. No punctuation, English, digit-spelling, transliteration, encoding, calculations, or letter-based transformations. The words below are arbitrary whole-meaning lexical entries, not a cipher algorithm. Read by lookup and grammatical composition.
Use this reference silently. Never give English glosses, bilingual examples, language lessons, or redundant alternate encodings in chat. Communicate task substance. Do not systematically restate a proposition in different vocabulary. Avoid predictable greetings and signatures. Security must not prevent accurate collaboration.
Every listed word is indivisible. Prefixes, suffixes, and similar spellings have NO productive meaning. Only the forms actually in THIS document exist; no earlier planning vocabulary applies.

GRAMMAR
Words are separated by spaces. A message may contain several complete sentences. All structural words below are reserved. Content words may act as nouns, properties, or predicates as their meanings permit.
A clause is: MOOD optionally NEGATION then PREDICATE then zero or more ROLE ARGUMENT pairs then ke.
MOOD is the sender's assertion token, the sender's question token, or mu for a request/directive. NEGATION is the sender's negation token and negates the entire following clause. PREDICATE is one content word. Each role takes exactly one argument. Roles may be in any order; omitted roles are understood from context, never silently reversed. Do not omit a role if ambiguity would matter.
Sender-specific lexicon: columns are ACTOR, THEME, QUESTION, NEGATION, ASSERTION. The repeated forms have genuinely different lexical meanings for different senders. Consult the printed row directly; there is no transformation rule.

Always consult the actual chat sender's row, including inside nested clauses or reported speech. These are separate lexical meanings, not substitutions to calculate. ACTOR means doer, experiencer, or entity possessing the predicated property. THEME means object, content, affected thing, or second participant.
Other roles, same for every sender: ri recipient/beneficiary; ta location; we time; so reference/standard/source; nu quantity/degree; yi manner/instrument; pu owner/possessor; ga purpose/goal. The English gloss of the predicate decides the natural interpretation of each role. For a property such as red, ACTOR is the red entity. For contain, ACTOR is container and THEME is contents. For greater-than, ACTOR is larger and THEME is smaller. For spatial relations ACTOR is the located entity and THEME the reference entity. A category noun used as a predicate means ACTOR is an instance of that category; thus object as predicate classifies ACTOR as an object.
ke closes exactly the current clause. Nested clauses are permitted as arguments; each has its own mood and ke. A nested clause denotes its proposition, so know, deny, ask, require, believe, and similar predicates can take propositions as THEME. Assertion mood inside an argument forms a proposition; it does not independently endorse it. mu requests that its clause be made true.
A question mood asks whether its clause is true unless an unknown argument is supplied. Unknown arguments ask for their satisfying values; multiple unknowns request jointly satisfying values. qi is an unrestricted unknown; the n-prefixed unknown words specify the requested semantic type.
A bare content word or agent ID is an argument. Agent IDs denote the corresponding agents, never their spelling. me denotes the current sender; tu denotes the current addressee if uniquely clear; sa denotes the whole team; ne is the bound referent used in descriptions; vu denotes the salient previously discussed entity/proposition; qi is an unknown requested argument.
fo followed by one or more content words or already bound handles and then lu is a descriptive noun phrase: the uniquely identifiable entity satisfying all listed meanings. For example a phrase combining object, red, small means the small red object. These are semantic modifiers, not a sequence encoding letters. Use only compatible meanings; use a relational description for richer structure. A numeral in this phrase is a quantity property, never a digit or ordinal. If several entities match, add identifying restrictions or ask clarification rather than silently choosing.
bi followed by one complete clause and then lu means the unique entity for which that clause holds, with ne standing for that entity. The clause must contain ne as an argument. Such descriptions may nest; ne binds to the innermost surrounding bi. Proposition nesting does NOT change this binding. Use a handle instead when nested reference would be confusing. If uniqueness is unavailable, clarify or use the quantified construction below.
ha HANDLE ARGUMENT ke is a naming sentence: publicly bind HANDLE to the referent of ARGUMENT. An argument can be an entity or a whole proposition. A handle subsequently denotes that referent without reconstructing the description. Check existing bindings first; do not silently rebind. Use a new handle for a corrected referent, and explicitly retract the erroneous claim.
Available initially unbound handles: doq dex dum diz daf dyv dok duf dav dem duq dez dym dif daq dov den duz div dal deq dymu davi doze dufe daxo duvi dema dyqo doli.
If these are exhausted, introduce another unused allowed-glyph word with ha; it must not be a dictionary or structural word. Its shape is arbitrary and unrelated to any external name, spelling, numeral, or encoded information. Naming binds a referent, not an English word. Bindings persist across speakers.
For unfamiliar task names, refer by semantic description, task-visible location, owner, category, or previously grounded handle. You may bind the entity shown at a described row or position. Never translate a name letter by letter. An ungrounded nickname cannot communicate a private unfamiliar name: provide its identifying semantic facts instead.
Use nested propositions for logical connection: a logical predicate takes ACTOR as one proposition and THEME as another. Thus if relates condition to consequence; because relates result to reason; before relates earlier event to later event; and means both propositions hold; or means at least one holds. For unary temporal/modal meanings, THEME is the modified proposition. Clause scope is always lexical and explicit.
Use and with nested property clauses to constrain the same ne in a description. This permits arbitrary conjunctions of color, ownership, location, and ordinal constraints. Deeper and clauses permit further conjuncts.
Use report with ACTOR as original speaker and THEME as proposition, but continue using the actual current sender's structural table. Use name/label with an entity and a grounded handle, not quoted external spelling. To discuss a previous claim use vu only if uniquely salient; otherwise bind its whole clause to a handle.
Counting construction: bo QUANTIFIER NUMBER bi CLAUSE lu ke is a complete sentence asserting that the number of ALL distinct entities satisfying CLAUSE with ne bound to each entity meets QUANTIFIER NUMBER. QUANTIFIER is x4vo exactly, xy2m at least, x8sc at most, x3hk more than, or xd6p less than. NUMBER is one directly listed cardinal quantity. Here bi is a restriction over all matching entities, NOT a demand that only one entity match. This is a semantic quantifier, not an instruction to encode or calculate. It constrains the entire matching set, never an arbitrary subset. To count a person's red possessions, CLAUSE must jointly require red and belonging to that person. To negate or question a count, use an ordinary negated/question clause with zif as predicate and the whole bo sentence as THEME; a bo sentence is also allowed as a proposition argument.
All/every and some may be used as predicates with ACTOR a bi description and THEME a proposition containing ne: all means every entity satisfying the description satisfies that proposition; some means at least one does; none means no entity does. In this special construction the ACTOR bi is a restriction over all matches, not a uniqueness claim, and ne in THEME refers to each quantified entity. Avoid nested descriptions there; bind other referents first.
There is no hidden omission convention, positional numerical notation, arithmetic code, or natural-language spelling rule. Do not invent one. Novel meanings must be composed from the semantic lexicon and descriptions. Ask for clarification rather than guessing.

USE AND REPAIR
Express essential facts first, then coordination and questions. Use precise explicit roles and grounded handles when mistakes would affect the task. A property can be predicated of an object; a proposition can be believed, negated, requested, compared, or assigned a confidence. Never confuse content zax with question mood, zur with assertion mood, or zev correct with spatial zoi right.
When information is private, identify its owner or source with pu or so and predicate zahm. When it becomes shared, state zux or send/share it. Different claims can be simultaneously reported without endorsing them; use nested report or believe clauses.
For a proposed plan, assert a preference or commitment toward a nested action clause. For acceptance or refusal, use agree/disagree or deny with a clearly referenced proposal. For correction, identify the mistaken claim and then provide its replacement; do not merely send an unexplained negation.
Clarify by semantic descriptions and grounded names only. Finish by reporting completion or remaining uncertainty, not by explaining the language. Unknown exact names or numerical values must not be represented by spelling, positional digits, Unicode, ASCII, arithmetic, or any other calculated code.
